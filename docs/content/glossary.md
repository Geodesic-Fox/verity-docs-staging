Each term has one meaning across these pages. An entry gives the meaning in brief and links to the section that defines it; a symbol, where a term has one, is given beside it. The program format's own terms, such as ports, elements and operations, are in [its table](/accounting/circuits#terms).

## Parties and protocols

- **Developer**: the party that runs computations in its datacenters, keeps private records of them as circuits and the values they computed, and proves properties of those records ([Overview](/)).
- **Auditor**: the party that checks the developer's proofs, with a challenger online and a verifier offline, and in the onsite protocol with devices at the developer's site ([Overview](/)).
- **Faithfulness**: the developer's records accurately and comprehensively reflect its actual computations. The onsite protocol's accept is faithfulness as Verity states it ([Protocols](/protocols)).
- **Inference-only**: the records reflect only having performed inference, not training. NCI is its policy ([NCI](/policies/nci)).
- **Protocol**: who the parties are, what each one trusts, how a run goes and what an accept means. Verity has two, remote and onsite ([Protocols](/protocols)).
- **Remote protocol** (`verity/remote/v0`): integrity and work, checked with no hardware of the auditor's at the developer's site ([Remote](/protocols/remote)).
- **Onsite protocol** (`verity/onsite/v0`): the remote protocol with the auditor's network certifiers and memory challengers at the site, which add memory and network accounting ([Onsite](/protocols/onsite)).
- **Subprotocol**: a protocol that a composed protocol runs as one of its parts: sampling for integrity, or compute, memory or network accounting. It states what is committed, its sampling design, its check and its public outputs, and the composed protocol owns everything else ([Composition](/protocols/architecture#composition)).
- **Composition**: how a protocol combines its subprotocols. Its commitment binds theirs, and it decides rejection first: any rejection rejects, `undecided` needs no rejection and some subprotocol undecided, and `accept` needs every one to accept ([Composition](/protocols/architecture#composition)).
- **Workers**: the developer's servers and GPUs. They serve, commit and run nearly all of the proving, the inner proofs with zero knowledge off, and neither party trusts them ([Roles](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#roles)).
- **ZK server**: the workers' only path to the auditor, trusted by the developer and running on CPUs it controls. It commits every inner message under a salt it draws and releases outer proofs under its contract, so zero knowledge holds whatever the workers do, short of stops and output freedom. In a subprotocol's statements the prover $\mathcal{P}$ is the workers behind it ([Roles](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#roles)).
- **The ZK server's contract**: each value the workers hand the ZK server leaves only as the kind the argument and the statement's shape give it: a commitment under a salt the ZK server draws, a value it recomputes, a value masked by a pad the workers never see, or a constant, with every relation checked on the unmasked message before any pad leaves, and each at its slot in a public schedule. The first value that fails stops the session ([The contract](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#the-contract)).
- **Challenger**: the auditor's online service. It commits to every salt before the run and opens each once, answers each proof message with fresh coins, draws only after it has logged what it draws against, and keeps the record. It never sees the witness ([Parties](/protocols/remote#parties)).
- **Verifier** ($\mathcal{V}$): the auditor's side, which runs a challenger online and checks the challenger's record offline. The offline check is C-Flock's verifier, a total, deterministic function written in Lean, which reads no clock and no randomness and returns accept or a reason for rejecting ([Parties](/protocols/remote#parties), [Verification phase](/proof-systems/c-flock#verification-phase)).
- **Live coins**: fresh coins the challenger draws in answer to each of the prover's messages, once it has recorded the message. In C-Flock's zero-knowledge mode it commits to all of a session's coins when the session opens, in a coin tree. No coin comes from a public beacon or from Fiat–Shamir ([Live coins](/proof-systems/randomness#live-coins)).
- **Network certifier**: a device of the auditor's on every uplink of every node, not trusted by the developer. It releases the node's traffic at a constant rate on a fixed grid and attests what crossed the link ([Network certifier](/accounting/network/certifier)).
- **Network certificate**: a network certifier's signed record of what crossed a link in one window. It never reaches the auditor in the clear: it is witness, and the developer proves that it passes its check ([Network certifier](/accounting/network/certifier#the-check)).
- **Spatial unit**: the machines behind the network certifiers, whose boundary the certifiers watch ([Placement](/protocols/onsite#placement)).
- **Memory challenger**: a device of the auditor's beside each node's network certifier, inside the node's isolation boundary. It runs memory accounting's timed rounds on its own clock and signs each session ([Proof of useful space](/accounting/memory/pous#challenge)).
- **Trusted base**: the parts each guarantee rests on, who trusts each part and what each must do. The workers are in neither party's ([Trusted base](/protocols/remote#trusted-base)).
- **Premise**: a fact about the deployment that a protocol rests on and no proof covers, such as the ZK server being the workers' only path out ([Premises](/protocols/remote#premises)).
- **Public outputs**: what a protocol makes public with an accept, each listed with its reason. Everything else stays hidden, and a reject or an undecided shows only the verdict and its reason ([Public outputs](/protocols/remote#public-outputs)).
- **Serving** and **proving**: serving is the live inference path, and its cost is serving overhead, such as committing values while serving; proving is proof generation, and its cost is proving overhead ([vLLM](/integrations/vllm#serving-overhead-and-proving-overhead)).

## Circuits

- **Circuit** ($C$): gates $0, \dots, N-1$ in topological order, each reading earlier gates. It describes logical computation, not a GPU schedule, a proof system or a sampling design ([The circuit model](/accounting/circuits#the-circuit-model)).
- **Gate** ($g$) and **width** ($w_g$): a node of a circuit, which holds a value of $w_g$ bits. An *input gate* reads nothing and takes its value from the inputs; every other gate is *computed* from the gates it reads, and a constant is a computed gate that reads nothing ([The circuit model](/accounting/circuits#the-circuit-model)).
- **Implements** and **contains**: a circuit $D$ implements $C$ when it produces $C$'s outputs from $C$'s inputs, computed however it likes, and contains $C$ when it also computes each of $C$'s computed gates with the same operation from the same wires ([The circuit model](/accounting/circuits#the-circuit-model)).
- **Program**: one circuit written compactly, as Definitions called from a root body, and named by its SHA-512 digest ([The program format](/accounting/circuits#the-program-format)).
- **Registered input** and **run input**: the two binding times of a program's input gates. A registered input is bound when the program is registered, as its weights are; a run input is bound per request ([Values and structure](/accounting/circuits#values-and-structure)).
- **Silicon**: the hardware's exact arithmetic. Circuits match it on every input bit pattern, NaN and infinity included ([Silicon](/hardware/accelerators#silicon)).
- **Subcircuit template** and **subcircuit**: a template is named, with typed parameters such as a GEMM coordinate's inner dimension and its numeric semantics; a subcircuit binds every parameter, and is what a proof system proves and a benchmark measures ([Subcircuit templates](/accounting/circuits#subcircuit-templates)).
- **Trace** ($\tau$): a value $\tau_g$ of $w_g$ bits for each gate; $\tau_A$ is its values at a set of gates $A$, in gate order ([Traces](/accounting/circuits#traces)).
- **Honest trace**: the evaluation of the circuit on its inputs, `evaluate(C, x)`. Evaluation is exact, so the honest trace is a function of the inputs alone ([Traces](/accounting/circuits#traces)).
- **Committed trace** ($\tau^\star$): the trace a commitment binds, fixed by the prover's strategy once it sends the commitment, and so before any sample is drawn ([Sampling](/proof-systems/sampling#assumptions)).
- **Partition** ($U$): a division of a circuit's computed gates into proof units, each gate in exactly one. The verifier derives it itself, by evaluating a named query on its own copy of the program ([Partitions](/accounting/circuits#partitions)).
- **Proof unit** ($u$): one part of a partition, proved on its own. It computes a function $f_u$ from its inputs to its outputs, and outputs at most 16 bits; its inputs are unbounded ([Proof units](/accounting/circuits#proof-units)).
- **Boundary gates** ($\partial U$): every input gate of the circuit, and the inputs and outputs of every proof unit. They are what serving commits and all that a unit's check reads ([Partitions](/accounting/circuits#partitions)).
- **Faulty unit**: a proof unit whose outputs in a trace aren't its function of its inputs, $\tau_{\mathrm{out}(u)} \neq f_u(\tau_{\mathrm{in}(u)})$. A unit fed wrong inputs that computes faithfully from them isn't faulty ([Partitions](/accounting/circuits#partitions)).
- **Faulty set** ($B^\star$): the proof units faulty in the committed trace ([Integrity](/proof-systems/sampling#integrity)).
- **Input unit**: the circuit's input gates, which are never drawn. The verifier checks each input commitment against its external anchor, such as the declared model's weights ([Proof units](/accounting/circuits#proof-units)).
- **Shape**: proof units whose standalone circuits are equal share a shape, and one lowering and one statement serve them all ([Proof units](/accounting/circuits#proof-units)).
- **Refines**: a finer partition refines a coarser one when each fine unit lies inside one coarse unit ([Proof units](/accounting/circuits#proof-units)).
- **Replay unit**: a part of a coarser partition that the proof units refine, which a prover that doesn't keep its trace replays to rebuild the values it proves ([Two-stage sampling](/proof-systems/sampling#two-stage-sampling)).

## Proof systems

- **Commit-and-prove argument**: a proof system that binds values with a commitment, then proves statements about the committed values on the verifier's live coins. Its methods are `commit`, `prove`, `verify` and `verify` as a circuit ([Proof systems](/proof-systems#methods)).
- **Construction**: a commit-and-prove argument that implements the methods with the properties sampling and zero knowledge need. [C-Flock](/proof-systems/c-flock) is the one documented here ([Proof systems](/proof-systems)).
- **Statement** ($x$): a circuit and a commitment that binds values at some of its gates. It says that some trace opens the commitment and that the circuit holds on it; the trace is the *witness* ([Statements](/proof-systems#statements)).
- **Commitment** (`com`): binds values before anything is proved about them. A later proof uses the values only through openings that the verifier checks against the commitment ([Commitments](/proof-systems/commitments#what-a-commitment-establishes)).
- **Hiding leaf**: a leaf whose published data hides the committed value, so that a low-entropy value can't be searched from its digest: `hm96-sha512/v1` ([Hiding leaves](/proof-systems/commitments#hiding-leaves)).
- **Knowledge soundness** ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ks}}$): when `verify` accepts, an extractor recovers values that satisfy the statement, except with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ks}}$ ([Properties](/proof-systems#properties)).
- **Binding** ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bind}}$): the extracted values agree with the committed trace on the statement's gates, except with probability $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{bind}}$ ([Properties](/proof-systems#properties)).
- **Hiding** ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hide}}$): a commitment reveals nothing of the values it binds, within $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hide}}$ ([Properties](/proof-systems#properties)).
- **Zero knowledge** ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zk}}$): every verifier's view can be simulated from what it may learn, the public parameters and the public outputs. Verity asks it against a malicious verifier ([Theorem](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#theorem)).
- **Sampling**: proving a random sample of a circuit's proof units, so that an accept bounds how many units are faulty, sampled or not ([Sampling](/proof-systems/sampling)).
- **Sampling design** ($\mathcal{D}$): a probability distribution over sets of proof units, from which the verifier draws its sample: `subset:K`, `bernoulli:p`, `stratified:K`, the default, or `work:K` ([Sampling designs](/proof-systems/sampling#sampling-designs)).
- **Stratum**: the proof units of one subcircuit template, from each of which `stratified:K` and `work:K` draw ([Sampling designs](/proof-systems/sampling#sampling-designs)).
- **Closure**: proof units proved along with the drawn ones, such as the units that compute the rows a drawn tile reads ([Sampling designs](/proof-systems/sampling#sampling-designs)).
- **Acceptance probability** ($P_a(B)$): the probability that the sample misses a set $B$ of proof units ([Integrity](/proof-systems/sampling#integrity)).
- **Confidence set** ($\mathcal{M}_\delta$): the sets of proof units that the sample misses with probability more than $\delta$, computed from the sampling design alone ([Integrity](/proof-systems/sampling#integrity)).
- **Integrity**: sampling's guarantee. An accept means the faulty set lies in the confidence set, except with probability at most $\delta + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ks}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{bind}}$ ([Integrity](/proof-systems/sampling#integrity)).
- **Integrity profile**: what an accept of integrity records: what was audited, the sampling design, how many units were drawn and the error terms. The bound on faulty units is read from it at any $\delta$ ([Integrity](/proof-systems/sampling#integrity)).
- **Two-stage sampling** and **lazy commitments**: the verifier draws replay units, then proof units inside each drawn one; with lazy commitments the prover commits each drawn replay unit's interior only after the first draw ([Two-stage sampling](/proof-systems/sampling#two-stage-sampling)).
- **Recursion**: the transformation of Ben-Or et al. that makes a public-coin protocol $\Pi$ zero-knowledge. The prover runs $\Pi$ unmodified and sends a hiding commitment in place of each message, then proves in zero knowledge that the committed messages make $V_\Pi$ accept at the coins sent ([Zero knowledge](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#protocol)).
- **Inner proof** and **outer proof**: the inner proofs are the wrapped protocol's, run with zero knowledge off, each message reaching the verifier only as a hiding commitment. The outer proof shows, in zero knowledge, that the committed messages make $V_\Pi$ accept at the coins sent ([Protocol](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#protocol)).
- **$\Pi$**, **$V_\Pi$** and **$\mathsf{Arg}$**: in zero knowledge, $\Pi = (P_\Pi, V_\Pi)$ is the wrapped protocol, $V_\Pi(x, m, c) = (b, o)$ its verifier written as a circuit, the statement of every outer proof, and $\mathsf{Arg} = (P_{\mathsf{Arg}}, V_{\mathsf{Arg}})$ the argument that proves it ([Public parameters](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#public-parameters)).
- **$P^*$** and **$V^*$**: any prover and any verifier, however it behaves: a cheating prover and a malicious verifier ([Parties](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#parties)).
- **Public circuit** and **private circuit**: the two settings of recursion. With a public circuit the statement $x$ is public, and the outer proof hides the trace; with a private circuit $x$ is committed and moved into the outer proof's witness, which hides the circuit too ([What's revealed](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#whats-revealed), [Private circuit](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#private-circuit)).
- **Circuit privacy**: hiding the circuit as well as the values, by registering it under a hiding commitment as a member of a public class ([Circuit privacy](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#circuit-privacy)).
- **Leakage**: what the auditor can learn beyond what zero knowledge bounds. The public outputs are revealed by design; under a ZK server the workers can also carry bits through stops, whether and where sessions stop, and through output freedom. A public schedule removes timing ([What changes](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#what-changes)).
- **Output freedom** ($\log_2 q_j$): the bits the workers carry through an output that $V_\Pi$ lets take $q_j$ values on accepting transcripts, zero when the statement and the coins fix it ([What changes](/proof-systems/zero-knowledge#what-changes)).

## Resource accounting

- **Resource accounting**: bounds on the resources a deployment used, its compute, memory and network, each a subprotocol of its own, checked against the hardware and circuits the developer declared ([Resource accounting](/accounting)).
- **Consumer** and **charge** ($y_u$): a consumer reads integrity, charging each faulty unit a nonnegative quantity $y_u$, such as its work or its output bits, by one of two rules, a total or exfiltration ([Reading integrity](/accounting/composition#reading-integrity)).
- **Compute accounting** (proof of useful work, PoUW): an accepted audit means that after a salt it couldn't predict, the prover spent at least a stated share, $(1-\gamma)(1-\varepsilon_s)W$, of the reference cost of the matrix multiplications it declared ([Proof of useful work](/accounting/compute/pouw)).
- **Tile**: one block of a declared matrix multiplication, rows of $A$ times rows of $B$ at full $k$, computed under noise derived from the salt. Tiles are compute accounting's proof units ([The protocol](/accounting/compute/pouw#compute)).
- **Scheme**: in compute accounting, how a tile is noised, computed and checked, with each tile's reference cost, the assumption the scheme rests on and its constants ([Schemes](/accounting/compute/pouw#schemes)).
- **Memory accounting** (proof of useful space, PoUS): timed rounds bound how much of an encoding of the committed weights a server holds when it is challenged ([Memory accounting](/accounting/memory)).
- **Encoding** ($C$): the form in which a server keeps a model's weights, decoding it on every use. A block it doesn't hold takes too long to rebuild within a round's deadline ([Memory accounting](/accounting/memory)).
- **Network accounting**: separate bounds on what a node's traffic can carry out, in its content and in its timing ([Network accounting](/accounting/network)).
- **Content bound**: network accounting's bound on what the traffic says. It reads integrity: a covert sender's freedom lies in faulty proof units, each charged its output bits ([Content bound](/accounting/network/content)).
- **Timing bound**: network accounting's bound on what a covert sender carries out through when frames leave. It rests on the network certifiers and holds on every accepted run ([Network certifier](/accounting/network/certifier#timing-bound)).
- **Output bits**: the gates a unit exports to other units or to the outputs, one bit each in a Boolean circuit ([Content bound](/accounting/network/content#the-charge)).

## Policies

- **Policy**: a condition on declarations that the verifier checks from the circuit and its baseline alone. It has no rounds of its own: it draws no coins, asks for no proofs and reads no values ([Policies](/policies/nci#policies)).
- **Declaration** ($(D, \theta)$): the circuit $D$ the developer registers, and its *baseline* $\theta$, the input gates whose values it committed beforehand, such as its models, code and data in storage ([Declarations](/policies/nci#declarations-and-novel-information)).
- **Novel information**: every value outside the baseline: the remaining input gates, such as prompts, and every computed gate ([Declarations](/policies/nci#declarations-and-novel-information)).
- **Isolation unit** (IU): one part of a division of a declared circuit's computed gates, a partition in the role of isolating what each part reads ([Declarations](/policies/nci#declarations-and-novel-information)).
- **Novel communication intensity** (NCI): the bits of novel information an isolation unit reads, per gate it holds. NCI's policy asks for isolation units of at most $\Gamma$ gates, each with NCI at most $\alpha$ ([The policy](/policies/nci#the-policy)).
- **Read cap** ($K$): the most novel information an isolation unit of a complying division reads, $K = \alpha\Gamma$ ([The policy](/policies/nci#the-policy)).

## Guarantees

- **Guarantee**: a theorem in which named assumptions imply a conclusion, with the error it carries ([Reading a page](/#reading-a-page)).
- **Assumption**: a named hypothesis that nothing proves. [Security assumptions](/assumptions) lists the cryptographic ones, with a rating of how far to trust each ([Reading a page](/#reading-a-page)).
- **Completeness**: an honest prover is accepted, except with the completeness error a page states.
- **Proved for the abstract scheme**, **proved and instantiated** and **Lean proof pending**: the status of a guarantee's proof sketch. Lean proves the statement over abstract components, or of the construction Verity runs, or the statement is given on the page and its Lean proof is not done ([Reading a page](/#reading-a-page)).
- **Census entry**: a hardware, model, workload or network entry with a stable id, which other pages cite instead of restating its numbers ([Contributing guidelines](/contributing#ids)).
