Resource accounting bounds how much of a deployment's compute, memory, memory bandwidth and network could have gone to something other than what the developer declared, its [hardware](/accounting/hardware) and its [circuits](/accounting/circuits). Each resource has two layers. A cryptographic protocol secures a bound on the declared use: the work spent, the bits held, the bytes read, or, for the network, the most a run's traffic can say beyond its output. A physical layer then takes the hardware's capacity, from datasheets, measurements and named assumptions about the device, and the headroom is the difference: what the hardware could have done that the audit doesn't account for. The [remote](/protocols/remote) and [onsite](/protocols/onsite) protocols compose them, and [composition](/accounting/composition) says how the bounds that read [sampling](/proof-systems/sampling)'s integrity guarantee share one accept.
